Why does a cutting machine reject a simple image?

A PNG or JPG is a grid of pixels. Cricut, Silhouette Cameo, and laser engravers don't cut colored dots: they follow paths. If you import a raster image as is, the software has to "guess" the outlines. The result is often irregular, with jagged edges, too many nodes, or areas the blade can't follow.

Converting the image to SVG solves the problem at the source. PixelToPath traces the shapes and produces clean vector paths - the format that Design Space, Silhouette Studio, LightBurn, and RDWorks expect. You get a crisp outline, at any scale, even if the original image was small or compressed.

What is a "cut-ready" SVG?

An SVG (Scalable Vector Graphics) describes shapes using mathematical curves, not pixels. For cutting, what matters isn't having a pretty picture on the screen: it's having closed paths, few unnecessary nodes, and clear contrast between the shape to cut and the background.

That's exactly what PixelToPath aims for with the Black & White preset. The benefits for crafting:

  • Crisp outlines: the blade or laser follows a smooth curve, not a staircase of pixels
  • Infinite scalability: enlarge a design for a tote bag or shrink it for a sticker without losing quality
  • Machine compatible: SVG is accepted by Cricut Design Space, Silhouette Studio, Brother ScanNCut, and LightBurn
  • Editable: open the file in Inkscape to weld, hollow out, or separate layers before cutting

What can you cut with a PixelToPath SVG?

Once your design is in SVG format, it opens in the most popular cutting software:

  • Adhesive vinyl and stickers: a PNG design becomes an SVG that Cricut Design Space can cut cleanly
  • HTV for textiles: import the file into Silhouette Studio and press it onto a tote bag, t-shirt, or sweatshirt
  • Laser cutting: wood, acrylic, leather, or cardboard - LightBurn and diode engravers follow vector paths
  • Paper and scrapbooking: stencils, invitations, cards, and embellishments with clean edges
  • Stencils and engraving: isolate the shape, invert it if needed, then engrave or cut the negative space

Whether you're using a Cricut Explore, Maker, Silhouette Cameo, or a laser engraver, the same clean SVG gets the job done.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Cricut Design Space accept SVGs created with PixelToPath?

Yes. Design Space imports SVG files natively. Download the SVG from PixelToPath, then use "Upload" in Cricut Design Space. The paths will appear as cut layers that you can resize, duplicate, or weld.

Which preset should I use for cutting?

The Black & White preset is the most suitable: it produces high contrast and closed outlines, ideal for vinyl, paper, and laser. The Poster mode is good if your design has multiple colors to cut into separate layers. Avoid Photo for mechanical cutting: too many details will break the path.

Is it compatible with Silhouette Cameo and laser cutting?

Yes. Silhouette Studio (Designer Edition or higher) opens SVGs. For laser cutting, import the same file into LightBurn, RDWorks, or your engraver's software. A vector path is the standard input format for these machines.

Can I convert a photo into a cut-ready SVG?

A detailed photo isn't great for cutting: too many gradients, too many small paths. Choose a high-contrast design instead - like a logo, silhouette, doodle, stamp, or clipart. If you really want to use a photo, the Poster preset simplifies colors, but line art will always be cleaner under the blade.

Is the converter really free - and can I vectorize any image from the web?

PixelToPath is 100% free, with no account or watermarks. However, vectorizing an image doesn't give you the rights to use it. Only cut designs that you created, that are royalty-free, or for which you have a license - copyrighted characters remain prohibited for sale.
